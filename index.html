What are CPP and OAS, and why do they matter here?
CPP — Canada Pension Plan
A pension you build up by working. The average person starting at 65 gets about $925 a month, not the $1,508 maximum you'll see quoted — that maximum assumes almost 40 years of contributions at the highest income level. We use the average, because it's closer to most people.
OAS — Old Age Security
Paid from age 65 based on how long you've lived in Canada, not on whether you worked. Roughly $742 a month at full residency.
Why it changes the answer
These cover the first slice of your retirement income, so your savings only need to cover the rest. Calculators that ignore them make the target look terrifying and put people off starting at all.

Plain-English guide to these accounts
TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account
Money goes in after tax. Everything it earns is yours, forever, tax-free. You can take it out any time for any reason. It is not a savings account — you can hold investments in it, and most people should.
RRSP — Registered Retirement Savings Plan
You get a tax refund for putting money in, then pay tax when you take it out in retirement. It works when you earn more now than you will later.
FHSA — First Home Savings Account
The best of both: a refund going in, and tax-free coming out if you buy a first home. $8,000 a year, $40,000 lifetime. Introduced in 2023, and badly under-used.
Employer match
If your employer adds money when you contribute, that is an instant return no investment can match. Always take all of it first.
